How Does UK VAT Work?

What is VAT?

Value Added Tax (VAT) is a consumption tax added to most goods and services sold in the UK. It's charged at each stage of the supply chain where value is added — hence the name. Businesses registered for VAT collect it on behalf of HMRC and can reclaim the VAT they pay on business purchases.

UK VAT Rates

  • Standard rate (20%) — Applies to most goods and services: electronics, clothing, professional services, furniture, cars, and general retail items.
  • Reduced rate (5%) — Applies to specific categories: domestic fuel and power, children's car seats, mobility aids for the elderly, and energy-saving materials for residential properties.
  • Zero rate (0%) — Applies to essential items: most food (not catering or hot takeaway), children's clothing, books and newspapers, public transport, and prescription medicines. Zero-rated goods are still VAT-taxable but at 0% — businesses can reclaim input VAT on related costs, unlike exempt supplies.

Adding vs Removing VAT

Adding VAT is straightforward: multiply the net price by (1 + rate). For example, £100 + 20% VAT = £120 gross. This is how businesses calculate the selling price from their net cost.

Removing VAT (also called "extracting VAT") works in reverse: divide the gross amount by (1 + rate). For a £120 gross price at 20%, the net is £120 ÷ 1.2 = £100, and the VAT portion is £20. This is how you work out the pre-VAT price from a VAT-inclusive total.

VAT Registration

UK businesses must register for VAT once their taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in any rolling 12-month period (threshold as of 2024/25). Once registered, you must charge VAT on taxable supplies, submit quarterly returns through Making Tax Digital (MTD), and keep digital records.

Voluntary registration is also possible below the threshold — it can be beneficial if you sell mainly to VAT-registered businesses (who can reclaim your VAT) and you want to reclaim VAT on your own purchases.
